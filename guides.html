Spanish learning guides

These guides explain the Spanish grammar and pronunciation behind the words in the game. Read one before or after you play. Start with the learning habits below if you want to get the most out of the game, then move on to grammar, sounds, and vocabulary in context.

Learning habits

Tips for learning vocabulary: ten practical habits for making new words stick, from writing down every word to quizzing yourself. Start with one habit and add another when the first feels easy, rather than trying all ten at once.

Immersion without moving: practical ways to find Spanish moments in an ordinary day, from labels and devices to listening and writing. The best moments are the ones you already have: labels around the house, a device set to Spanish, or a few minutes of listening each day.

Grammar

Ser vs. estar: the two verbs for “to be,” with the DOCTOR and PLACE memory tricks and adjectives that change meaning. If you remember one rule, make it this: ser is for what something is, estar is for how something is or where it is.

Saber vs. conocer: the two verbs for “to know,” facts and skills versus people and places. A quick test: if you could put “how to” in front of it, it is saber; if you could put “I have met” in front of it, it is conocer.

Gustar and encantar: how to say what you like and love, and the other verbs that work the same way. The trick is that the thing you like is the subject, so the verb agrees with it, not with you: you say me gusta el libro but me gustan los libros.

Spanish articles: el, la, un, una, lo, al and del, and when Spanish uses or leaves out an article. When in doubt, learn every noun with its article — el libro rather than just libro — because gender is part of the word.

Conjugation and the infinitive: how endings work, when the infinitive stays unchanged, and a first look at past and future tenses. The infinitive is the form to learn first, because every other form is built from it, and it is the form you will find in a dictionary.

Verb Conjugation (Conjugación): practice present, preterite, and future forms for 113 common verbs — pick the right form, hear it, and say it, with spelling notes when a form changes. Start with the present tense, then add the preterite and future once the present forms feel solid.

El and la: Spanish noun gender: why nouns are masculine or feminine, patterns that help, and exceptions to watch for. Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine, but the exceptions — like el mapa and la mano — are worth learning on their own.

Spanish pronouns: gustar-type verbs, direct and indirect objects, reflexives, prepositions, possessives, and demonstratives, with a 700-sentence practice tool. The pronoun is usually pinned by the sentence itself, so you rarely need the translation to choose.

Por vs. para: the two little words that both mean "for" — por is the cause, the way, or the path, and para is the purpose, the destination, or the deadline — with a 100-sentence practice tool.

Sounds and spelling

Accents and pronunciation: the sounds of Spanish letters, where the stress falls, and how accent marks work. Spanish spelling is mostly phonetic, so once you know the letter sounds and the stress rules, you can pronounce almost any word you see.

Dictation (Dictado): listening practice — hear a word, a verb form, or an expression and pick what you heard from four similar choices, across vocabulary, verbs, and idioms. The four choices are always similar, so you have to listen closely rather than guess from the first sound.

Vocabulary in context

Cognates: Spanish words you already know: words that look alike in English and Spanish, with patterns to help you spot hundreds of them. Watch for patterns like -tion becoming -ción, and a word you have never studied can suddenly look familiar.

Spanish-speaking countries and directions: the countries in the game and how to say up, down, left, and right. The game has 22 country cards, so this guide doubles as a map of the words you will meet while playing.

Common mistakes English speakers make: false friends, gender errors, ser versus estar, and more. The fastest way past a mistake like these is to learn the Spanish pattern as a whole phrase, not word by word.

Common expressions / Idioms (Modismos): 135 everyday Spanish expressions with a two-way flashcard tool, plus why idioms matter for sounding natural. Learn a few of these and your Spanish starts to sound like something people actually say, not something translated word for word.
