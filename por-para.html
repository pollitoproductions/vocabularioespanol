Por vs. para

Por and para cause more trouble than almost any other two words in Spanish, and it is easy to see why. In English, both of them often come out as "for." "This gift is for you" is para. "I bought it for twenty dollars" is por. Same English word, two completely different Spanish words. The trick is that por and para do not really mean "for." Each one has its own job, and once you learn the jobs, the choice gets much easier. This guide walks through the main uses of each one, with the traps students actually fall into.

Por is the cause, para is the purpose

The single most useful question to ask is this: am I talking about the reason something happened, or the goal I am aiming at? Por looks backward at the cause. Para looks forward at the purpose.

"No pude ir por la lluvia." I could not go because of the rain. The rain is the reason. It already happened, and it is why I stayed home. That is por.

"Estudio para aprender español." I study in order to learn Spanish. Learning Spanish is the goal ahead of me. It has not happened yet. That is para.

So if you can ask "why?" and the answer is a reason that already exists, use por. If you can ask "what for?" and the answer is a goal you want to reach, use para. "Por la lluvia" (because of the rain) against "para aprender" (in order to learn) is the pair to remember. A quick test: try replacing the word with "because of." If the sentence still makes sense, it is por. Try replacing it with "in order to." If that fits, it is para. Most of the time only one of those replacements works, and that tells you the answer.

This is also why "Gracias por tu ayuda" uses por. You are thankful because of the help. The help is the cause of your thanks, not a goal you are aiming at. And it is why "Estudio para sacar buenas notas" uses para. Good grades are the goal ahead, not the reason you already studied.

How long something lasts, or when it is due

Por and para both talk about time, but in opposite ways. Por tells you how long something lasts. Para tells you the point in time something is due.

"Estudio español por dos horas al día." I study Spanish for two hours a day. Two hours is a length of time, a stretch. That is por.

"Vivimos en Chile por tres años." We lived in Chile for three years. Three years is a length of time. Por again.

"La tarea es para el lunes." The homework is due Monday. Monday is a point on the calendar, a deadline. That is para.

"Necesito el informe para el viernes." I need the report by Friday. Friday is the deadline. Para.

The trap here is that English uses "for" for both. "I studied for two hours" (por) and "the homework is for Monday" (para) sound the same in English, but in Spanish they are different words. Ask yourself: am I measuring a stretch of time, or naming a day something is due? A stretch is por. A due date is para. If the time is a number of hours, days, or years, it is almost always por. If the time is a named day or date, it is almost always para.

Through and past, or to and for

Por is the road you travel on. Para is where you are headed.

"Caminamos por el parque." We walk through the park. The park is the place we are moving through. That is por.

"El tren cruza por las montañas." The train crosses through the mountains. The mountains are what it passes through. Por.

"Pasamos por tu casa ayer." We passed by your house yesterday. We went past it. Por.

"Salimos para México mañana." We leave for Mexico tomorrow. Mexico is the destination. That is para.

"Este tren va para Barcelona." This train goes to Barcelona. Barcelona is where it is headed. Para.

"Regresamos para casa después del cine." We return home after the movie. Home is the destination. Para.

Para also marks the person something is for. "Este regalo es para ti." This gift is for you. "Tengo un regalo para ti." I have a gift for you. "La maestra guardó un libro para Ana." The teacher saved a book for Ana. The person who receives something is always para, never por. Por with a person usually means "because of" or "on behalf of," which is a different idea entirely. So when something is going to a person or a place, that is para. When something is moving through, along, or past a place, that is por.

How you did it, what it cost, or what it is for

Por tells you how something was done, or what it cost. Para tells you what something is for, its intended use.

"Te llamo por teléfono." I call you by phone. The phone is the means, the way you did it. That is por.

"Lo mandé por correo." I sent it by mail. The mail is the means. Por.

"Lo compré por veinte dólares." I bought it for twenty dollars. Twenty dollars is the price. Por.

"Vendí mi bicicleta por cien dólares." I sold my bike for a hundred dollars. The price again. Por.

"Compré una mesa para la cocina." I bought a table for the kitchen. The kitchen is what the table is for, its intended use. That is para.

"Necesito gafas para leer." I need glasses to read. Reading is what the glasses are for. Para.

"Compré crema para la piel." I bought cream for the skin. The skin is what the cream is for. Para.

The difference is simple: por looks at how you did it or what you paid. Para looks at what the thing is meant for. "Por teléfono" is how you called. "Para la cocina" is what the table is for. If you can ask "how?" or "how much?" the answer is por. If you can ask "what is it for?" the answer is para.

Two more uses of por worth knowing

First, the passive voice. When something was done by someone, that someone takes por. "El libro fue escrito por Cervantes." The book was written by Cervantes. "La carta fue escrita por mi abuela." The letter was written by my grandmother. "El puente fue construido por ingenieros." The bridge was built by engineers. In English that is "by," and in Spanish it is always por. If you see "fue" plus a past participle, the person who did it will be introduced by por.

Second, going to get something. "Voy por pan a la tienda." I go to the store for bread. I am going to fetch it and bring it back. "Salió por leche al mercado." He went out to the market for milk. "Mi mamá fue por el periódico." My mom went for the newspaper. When you go somewhere to bring something back, that is por. Be careful, though: "voy para la tienda" means something else. That is going to the store as a destination, not going to fetch something. "Voy por pan" is fetching bread. "Voy para la tienda" is heading to the store. Same verb, different word, different meaning.

Common mistakes

Wrong: "Voy por aprender español." Right: "Voy para aprender español." Learning is a purpose, a goal ahead of you, so it is para. "Por aprender" is not how you say "in order to learn."

Wrong: "La tarea es por el lunes." Right: "La tarea es para el lunes." Monday is a deadline, a point in time. Por is for a length of time, not a due date.

Wrong: "Este regalo es por ti." Right: "Este regalo es para ti." The person who receives something is always para. Por with a person means "because of" or "on behalf of," which is not what you mean here.

Wrong: "Estudio español para dos horas." Right: "Estudio español por dos horas." Two hours is a length of time, so it is por. Para would mean the studying is due in two hours, which is not the idea at all.

Wrong: "Gracias para tu ayuda." Right: "Gracias por tu ayuda." Thanks are given because of the help. The help is the cause of your thanks, so it is por. "Gracias por" is the fixed phrase, and it is por because the help is the reason you are thankful.
