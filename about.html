About Pollito Productions

Pollito Productions makes free Spanish games and learning tools that run right in your browser. No downloads, no accounts, no sign-ups. Everything here is built around speaking: across the games and tools there are over 5,000 speaking and listening activities — words spoken aloud to collect them, answers spoken in the quizzes and tools, and listening in dictation and the Hear buttons. I am Brant Fuller, a Spanish teacher, and I built these games for my own students first.

How it started

This game began with my students. I am a teacher, and the many students I have taught are the reason it exists. Watching them work through vocabulary, I kept coming back to the same thought: memorizing a list of words is one of the least exciting ways to learn a language, and yet the words are what everything else is built on.

I wanted something that felt more like exploring than studying. A place where a word isn’t just a line on a worksheet, but something you walk up to, pick up, and keep. So I started building a world where learning Spanish vocabulary means wandering around, finding things, and collecting them.

I built it on my own, one piece at a time, and my students’ curiosity and questions, across all the groups I teach, kept shaping what went into it. The idea that you can see a picture and the word together, and come back to the ones you’ve collected, came straight from wanting to give them a better way to practice.

Vocabulario Español

A calm, open planet where you explore, find glowing vocabulary cards, and say each word aloud as you collect it. Each card shows a picture, the Spanish word with its article (el or la), and the English meaning. Simple words sit close to where you start, and less common vocabulary waits farther out, so beginners and more experienced learners share the same game. Your collection goes into an inventory you can review at any time, your progress is saved in your own browser, and the game has relaxed background music you can turn down or off. Best for a relaxed first game and classroom use.

Backrooms: El Glosario Perdido

A spookier first-person hunt through endless yellow hallways for the lost pages of your glossary. Find a word, read it aloud, and it restores your vida, your health. You mark the halls with a spray can so you can find your way back, and when the glossary reaches 100% you un-noclip back to reality. If your health runs out you simply wake up and keep going, so there is no hard game over. Heads up: this one has a spooky atmosphere and creatures called entities — preview it yourself before using it with younger students.

Generation Ship: ARK-7

Aboard the ARK-7, a great spinning space station on its way to a new star. It stopped to mine in an asteroid field, but there was an accident, and its Spanish repositories were lost — words and exercises scattered into space. Walk the decks, climb into a shuttle, and fly out to recover them, one spoken word at a time. Inside the ship, nine learning-tool stations wait in the agriculture ring, and a quest guides you as you rebuild each lost repository.

Learning tools

Twelve learning tools cover the words from every angle. Practice has quizzes, flashcards, and an El or la? game. Cognates covers 487 Spanish-English cognates in flashcards and lists, with audio in both languages. Verb Conjugation drills 113 verbs in the present, preterite, and future. Ser vs. estar has 100 questions on the two verbs for 'to be.' Modismos covers 135 everyday Spanish idioms. Dictation has you listen and pick the word you heard. False friends covers 100 look-alike words that mean something different in Spanish. Saber vs. conocer sorts out the two verbs for 'to know.' Pronouns drills 820 sentences across seven categories. Por vs. para has 100 sentences on the two prepositions. Tú commands covers 100 affirmative and negative commands. The vocabulary list shows every one of the 1,000 words. Speaking practice in the tools gives feedback only and never blocks you.

Guides and worksheets

The guides explain the grammar behind the words: ser vs. estar, verb conjugation, articles, accents and pronunciation, and more.

What you learn

The site is built around a core of 1,000 Spanish words. Spanish Dash and Slope Español draw on their own word lists as well. Nouns appear with their article (el or la), so you learn the gender along with the word, and verbs appear in the infinitive, such as caminar (to walk). Categories include directions, countries, verbs, and everyday objects. But it is not just 1,000 words to memorize: across the games and tools there are over 5,000 speaking and listening activities — words spoken aloud to collect them in the ship, spoken answers in the conjugation, cognates, modismos, and practice tools, and listening in dictation and the Hear buttons. You can see every one of the 1,000 words on the vocabulary list.

Who it is for

Everything here is for anyone learning Spanish: students in school, adults picking the language up for travel or work, and parents or teachers looking for something that makes vocabulary practice feel less like homework. It does not assume any prior knowledge. A complete beginner can start with the simple words first, while someone with more experience can use the games and tools to fill gaps and meet less common words. Because everything is free and runs in the browser, there is nothing to install and no account to create. Teachers looking for something students actually want to play will find it here too: the games are made for the classroom but fun enough for home, so play on the classroom board, hand out a worksheet for work away from the screen, or let students explore on their own devices.

How to get the most from it

The games and tools work best as one part of a routine rather than the whole routine. Play for a short while, write down the words you collected, say them out loud, and then read one of the guides to see how those words fit into real sentences. The guides explain the grammar behind the vocabulary, such as noun gender, verb endings, and the difference between ser and estar, so the words you collect turn into things you can actually say.

Where should you start?

It depends on where you are. If you're brand new to Spanish, start with the planet game — wander, collect words, and say them aloud. If you already know some words, open the vocabulary list to see and hear them all, then try the practice tool to see what stuck. From there, pick the learning tools that match your level — cognates and false friends, conjugation, ser vs. estar, saber vs. conocer, pronouns, por vs. para, tú commands — or jump into the generation ship, which puts those same tools inside a 3D space station. That loop, play first and practice second, is how the whole site is meant to work.
