Gustar and encantar: saying what you like

One of the first things learners want to say is “I like it.” In Spanish, gustar is the verb for that, but it does not work the way “like” does in English. Once you understand the small shift in thinking, it opens up a whole group of useful verbs, including encantar (to love).

The big idea: it pleases me

Instead of “I like the book,” Spanish says something closer to “the book is pleasing to me”: Me gusta el libro. The thing you like is the grammatical subject, and the person who likes it is shown with a small pronoun. That is why the verb agrees with what you like, not with who does the liking.

The pronouns

  • me: to me (Me gusta la música.)
  • te: to you (¿Te gusta el café?)
  • le: to him, her, or you formal (Le gusta el fútbol.)
  • nos: to us (Nos gusta viajar.)
  • os: to you all, used in Spain (¿Os gusta la playa?)
  • les: to them or you all (Les gustan los animales.)

Singular or plural?

The verb has only two forms in everyday use, gusta and gustan. Use gusta when what you like is one thing, and gustan when it is more than one: Me gusta la pizza. Me gustan las pizzas.

Liking an action

When you like doing something, use the infinitive and keep the verb singular, even if you list several activities: Me gusta caminar. Me gusta leer y escribir. The infinitive acts as one idea.

Adding who: a mí, a ti, a ella

To emphasize or clarify who likes something, add a plus a person at the start of the sentence. The pronoun stays too: A mí me gusta el té, pero a ti te gusta el café. A María le gustan los perros. A mis amigos les gusta bailar. This is very useful for le and les, which could mean several people.

Encantar: love it

Encantar works exactly like gustar, but it is stronger: Me encanta la música. Me encantan los perros. Nos encanta viajar. Because the word already means “love,” you usually do not add mucho to it.

How much you like something

  • Me gusta mucho el chocolate. I like chocolate a lot.
  • Me gusta un poco. I like it a little.
  • No me gusta. I do not like it. Put no before the pronoun.
  • No me gusta nada. I do not like it at all.

Other verbs that work the same way

  • Interesar: Me interesa la historia.
  • Fascinar: Les fascinan los planetas.
  • Molestar: Me molesta el ruido.
  • Importar: No me importa.
  • Faltar: Me falta un lápiz. (I am missing a pencil.)
  • Doler: Me duele la cabeza. (My head hurts.)

Common mistakes

  • Wrong: Yo gusto el libro. Right: Me gusta el libro. The book is the subject, so the verb is not conjugated for yo.
  • Wrong: Me gusta los perros. Right: Me gustan los perros. Plural things need gustan.
  • Careful: Me gustas means “I like you” in the sense of attraction. To say you like a friend as a person, me caes bien is more natural.

A practice idea

Each time you collect a noun in the game, say whether you like it: Me gusta el libro. No me gustan las arañas. Within a few days, the pattern will feel automatic.
