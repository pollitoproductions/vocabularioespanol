Spanish pronouns

Pronouns are the small words that do a lot of work in Spanish — and they are one of the hardest things to get right, because the same little word can mean different things in different places. This tool works through them one category at a time, starting with gustar-type verbs, then direct objects, reflexive pronouns, and more.

The categories

  • Gustar-type: me, te, le, nos, les with verbs like gustar, encantar, fascinar — "I like it" is really "it pleases me."
  • Direct object: lo, la, los, las — the person or thing receiving the action.
  • Reflexive: me, te, se, nos — actions you do to yourself, like getting up or washing.
  • Indirect object: le, les — the person you give something to or tell something to.
  • After prepositions: mí, ti, sí, conmigo, contigo — pronouns that follow a, con, para, sin and more.
  • Possessive: mío, tuyo, suyo — "mine, yours, his/hers."
  • Demonstrative: este, ese, aquel — "this one, that one, that one over there."

How to use it

Pick a category at the top of the tool, read the sentence, and choose the pronoun that fits. Use Show translation whenever you want it. After you answer, you will see the full sentence with a short rule note — and you can hear it and say it yourself.
