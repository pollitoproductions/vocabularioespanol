Spanish pronouns

Pronouns are small words — me, te, lo, se, le, mí, mío, este — and they do a surprising amount of work in Spanish. They are also one of the hardest things to get right, because the same little word can mean different things in different places, and Spanish puts pronouns where English never would. This guide works through them one group at a time, with the sentences you will actually say.

Gustar-type: me, te, le, nos, les

Start here, because gustar turns English upside down. "I like the book" is not yo gusto el libro. In Spanish, the book is doing the pleasing, and you are the one being pleased: Me gusta el libro — literally, "the book pleases me." The thing you like is the subject, and the pronoun tells you who feels it.

The pronouns are me, te, le, nos, les — plus os for vosotros, optional in the tool. The verb agrees with the thing liked, not the person: one thing (or an infinitive) takes the singular form, more than one takes the plural.

  • Me gusta el libro. I like the book.
  • Me gustan los perros. I like dogs.
  • A María le encanta la música. María loves music.
  • Nos falta tiempo. We are short on time.
  • Me duele la cabeza. My head hurts.

The same pattern works with a whole family of verbs: gustar (to like), encantar (to love), fascinar (to fascinate), interesar (to interest), molestar (to bother), doler (to hurt), faltar (to need). When it is not clear who the pronoun refers to, Spanish adds a clarifier at the front: A mí me gusta, A vosotros os gusta.

One warning: Me gustas does not mean "I like it." It means "I like you" — I am attracted to you. The singular gusta goes with a thing; gustas goes with a person.

Direct object: lo, la, los, las

A direct object is the person or thing receiving the action of the verb. In English it comes after the verb — "I see him." In Spanish the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb: Lo veo, not veo lo. The forms are lo (him, it — masculine), la (her, it — feminine), los and las (them), chosen by the gender and number of the person or thing.

  • Lo veo todos los días. I see him (or it) every day.
  • La conozco bien. I know her well.
  • ¿Los tienes? Do you have them?
  • Las veo en la escuela. I see them at school.

Reflexive: me, te, se, nos

Reflexive pronouns are for actions you do to yourself: getting up, washing, getting dressed, sitting down. The verb carries a -se on the end in its dictionary form — levantarse, lavarse, vestirse. The forms are me, te, se, nos (and os for vosotros). The tricky one is se: it covers él, ella, usted and also ellos, ellas, ustedes, so the verb ending shows singular or plural.

  • Me levanto a las siete. I get up at seven.
  • Te lavas las manos. You wash your hands.
  • Se viste rápido. He gets dressed quickly. (Or: she gets dressed quickly.)
  • Nos sentamos aquí. We sit down here.
  • Ellos se duchan por la mañana. They shower in the morning.

Indirect object: le, les

An indirect object is the person you give something to or tell something to. The pronouns are le (to him, to her, to you — formal) and les (to them). Like direct objects, they go before the conjugated verb.

  • Le doy el libro a Ana. I give the book to Ana.
  • Les escribo una carta. I write them a letter.
  • Le digo la verdad. I tell him the truth. (Or: I tell her the truth.)
  • Le compro flores a mi madre. I buy flowers for my mother.

Here is the rule students get wrong most often: le stays singular even when the thing is plural. Le doy los libros means "I give him the books" — le is singular because there is one recipient, even though los libros is plural. Use les only when there is more than one recipient.

After prepositions: mí, ti, sí, conmigo, contigo

After a preposition — a, con, para, sin, de — Spanish uses a different set of pronouns: mí, ti, sí (and él, ella, nosotros, ellos). The two to memorize are the ones that change shape after con: conmigo (with me) and contigo (with you). Never say con mí or con ti.

  • Este regalo es para mí. This gift is for me.
  • Voy contigo. I am going with you.
  • ¿Vienes conmigo? Are you coming with me?
  • Hablo de ti. I am talking about you.
  • Sin ti, no puedo. Without you, I cannot.

Sí is the reflexive one — it refers back to the subject: Piensa en sí mismo (He thinks about himself).

Possessive: mío, tuyo, suyo

Possessive pronouns stand in for a thing you own: "mine, yours, his, hers, theirs." The forms are mío, tuyo, suyo (and nuestro, vuestro for "ours" and "yours" plural). The surprise: the pronoun agrees in gender and number with the thing owned, not the owner.

  • El libro es mío. The book is mine.
  • La casa es suya. The house is his. (Or: hers. Or: theirs.)
  • ¿Es tuyo este lápiz? Is this pencil yours?
  • Los zapatos son míos. The shoes are mine.
  • La idea es tuya. The idea is yours.

La casa es suya uses suya because casa is feminine, even if the owner is a man. And suyo can mean his, hers, theirs, or yours (formal) — only the context tells you which.

Demonstrative: este, ese, aquel

Demonstrative pronouns point at things: "this one, that one, that one over there." Spanish has three distances, where English has two. Este is near me, ese is near you, aquel is far from both of us.

  • Este es mío, ese es tuyo. This one is mine, that one is yours.
  • ¿Te gusta este libro o aquel? Do you like this book or that one over there?
  • Ese coche es nuevo. That car is new.
  • Aquella casa es muy vieja. That house over there is very old.
  • Este lápiz escribe bien. This pencil writes well.

Like possessives, demonstratives agree with the noun they point to: este, esta, estos, estas; ese, esa, esos, esas; aquel, aquella, aquellos, aquellas. They keep that agreement when they stand alone: Este es mío.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong: Yo gusto el libro. Right: Me gusta el libro. The thing is the subject; you are the one who feels it.
  • Wrong: Me gusta los perros. Right: Me gustan los perros. Plural things take gustan.
  • Wrong: Les doy el libro a Ana. Right: Le doy el libro a Ana. One recipient takes le, even if the thing is plural.
  • Wrong: Voy con mí. Right: Voy contigo. After con, "with me" is conmigo and "with you" is contigo.
  • Wrong: La casa es mío. Right: La casa es mía. It agrees with the thing owned — casa is feminine.
