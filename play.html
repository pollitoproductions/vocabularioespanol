Learn Spanish through play

Pollito Productions makes free browser games that teach Spanish vocabulary the way games teach everything else: you explore, you find things, and you keep what you find.

All three games below use the same 1,000 vocabulary cards. Each card shows a picture, the Spanish word with its article (el or la), and the English meaning. You walk up to a card, collect it, and say the word out loud. Nothing needs to be downloaded, there is no account, and your progress is saved in your own browser so you can pick up where you stopped.

Vocabulario Español

You are the keeper of a great glossary. One stormy night its pages came loose and scattered across a quiet planet as glowing vocabulary cards, hidden among the trees, shores, and hills. Explore the planet, find every card, and read each word aloud as you collect it. When all 1,000 cards are back in your book, the glosario is complete.

The world is round and open, so you can wander in any direction. Simple everyday words sit close to where you start, and less common vocabulary waits farther out, which lets beginners and more experienced learners share the same game. You can run, levitate, and cast a couple of playful spells, and every card you collect goes into an inventory you can review at any time from the menu. It plays with a keyboard on a computer or with touch controls on a phone or tablet.

Best for: a relaxed first game, classroom use, and learners who like to explore at their own pace.

Backrooms: El Glosario Perdido

You are walking through an industrial park at night when you kick a can of spray paint, pick it up, take two steps, and fall straight through the concrete. You have noclipped into the Backrooms: endless buzzing hallways, damp carpet, no sky, and a lot of yellow. In your pocket are a spray can, a lighter, and your old Spanish glosario, whose pages tore loose in the fall and now drift through the halls as glowing vocabulary cards.

Find a word, read it aloud, and it restores your vida, your health. Restore the glossary to 100% and you un-noclip, slipping back out of the Backrooms and into reality. Because every hall looks the same, you use the spray can to tag the walls and mark junctions, dead ends, and the places where you found cards, and your marks are saved with your game. A flamethrower deals with the rare entities that wander the halls. If your health runs out you simply wake up and keep going, so there is no hard game over.

Heads up: this one has a spooky atmosphere and creatures called entities. Preview it yourself before using it with younger students.

Best for: older learners and anyone who wants a challenge with more atmosphere.

Generation Ship: ARK-7

You are aboard the ARK-7, a great spinning space station on its way to a new star. It stopped to mine in an asteroid field, but there was an accident, and its Spanish repositories were lost — words and exercises scattered into space. Walk the decks, climb into a shuttle, and fly out to recover them, one spoken word at a time.

Inside the ship, six learning-tool stations wait in the agriculture ring: Cognates, Practice, Conjugation, Idioms, Dictation, and Ser vs. estar. Outside, vocabulary and question tiles drift among the stars, and a quest guides you as you rebuild each lost repository. Speak a word aloud to collect it, and mine asteroids for water and metal along the way.

Best for: learners who want the biggest adventure, and anyone ready to fly after the planet and the Backrooms.

How the games teach

All three games turn vocabulary into something you do, not something you stare at. Finding a card takes a little effort, which makes the word easier to remember than one read from a list. Seeing the picture next to the word links the Spanish to a meaning without going through English first, and saying each word aloud connects what you read to how it sounds. Because the card set is the same in all three games, words you meet in one carry over to the others, and you can switch whenever you want a change of scenery.

Nouns always appear with their article, so you learn gender along with the word, and verbs appear in the infinitive, the form you will find in any dictionary. Both of these ideas are explained in the guides, in El and la: Spanish noun gender and Spanish verbs and infinitives.

Which one should I start with?

If you are new to the games, begin with Vocabulario Español. It is calm and bright, nothing chases you, and it works well on a classroom Chromebook or a phone. Try Backrooms when you want a bit more tension and a reason to keep moving, or when you have already collected a good number of cards and want a fresh setting for the same words. When you are ready for the biggest adventure, board the Generation Ship: walk a spinning station, fly a shuttle, and rebuild the lost repositories one spoken word at a time.

Get more out of every session

A game session is most useful when something happens before and after it. Keep a notebook beside you and write down each word with its article, say it aloud, and quiz yourself afterward by covering one column. The tips for learning vocabulary go through ten habits like these, immersion without moving shows how to bring Spanish into an ordinary day, and the full vocabulary list lets you look up any word from any of the games.
