Section 1: Present

The present tense is the tense you live in: what you do, what you like, what is happening right now. Regular verbs take endings by family: -ar verbs take -o, -as, -a, -amos, -an; -er verbs take -o, -es, -e, -emos, -en; -ir verbs take -o, -es, -e, -imos, -en. (These are the Latin-American forms, no vosotros, as in the Verb Conjugation tool.)

Section 2: Preterite

The preterite is the tense for finished past actions: what you did yesterday, what happened last year, how the story ended. Regular endings: -ar verbs take -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron; -er and -ir verbs take -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -ieron. A few spelling changes protect pronunciation (buscar → busqué), but the sound stays the same.

Section 3: Future

The future is the tense for what comes next: plans, promises, predictions. It is the easiest tense to learn: every regular verb takes the same endings on the infinitive — -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án — and stem-changing verbs do not change their stems in the future at all.

Section 4: Irregulars

The verbs you use most are the least regular, and that is not bad luck: words people say every day wear down into irregular shapes in every language. Ser, estar, ir, tener, and hacer have to be learned as whole tables, not derived from rules. One shared form to know: ser and ir have the same preterite (fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fueron) — context tells you whether it means “was” or “went.”
