Free classroom resources: worksheets and packets

Free printable packs to go with the games and learning tools, made by a classroom teacher for his own students and shared with anyone who can use it. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to pay. Every packet below prints from your browser: open it, print it, and hand it out. Each packet has a name and date line at the top.

The packs are written for novice learners in grades 3 to 8 and for Spanish 1. A packet can carry a single class period or stretch into a longer unit, and the tools on this site give students a way to hear, speak, and check every answer after the paper work is done.

The games packet: one packet, two games

The planet game and Backrooms share the same 1,000 words, so they share this packet. Seventeen worksheets send students back into the game to find and collect words, then practice them on paper. Topics include the el and la gender rules, numbers and math, countries, colors, animals, verbs, jobs, food, weather and nature, directions, and building simple sentences. There is also a scavenger hunt, a short quiz, and a reflection page for the end of a lesson. Inside the packet you will find a mission briefing that teaches the controls, a collector's log for recording every card, practice on el and la, numbers and math, countries (including a word search), colors, animals, verbs sorted by their -ar, -er, and -ir endings, jobs in masculine and feminine forms, food groups, weather and nature, directions on a grid, and two sentence patterns (Quiero and Me gusta). It closes with a scavenger hunt, a 20-point quiz, and a reflection page, so one packet can carry a single lesson or a five-day mini-unit.

Learning-tools packets

The tools on this site hold far more than the games' word list, so each tool has its own packet. The exercises use the tools' real content: the same cognates, verbs, idioms, and ser-versus-estar questions students meet on screen, now as paper practice with no device needed. Each packet opens with a briefing, works through several different activities, and closes with a review quiz and a reflection, so it can carry a full lesson on its own. Print the packet, work it in class, then send students to the matching tool to hear the words spoken and check their speaking.

Before your first lesson

Try the game and the tools on the devices your students will actually use, whether that is laptops, Chromebooks, tablets, or phones, so there are no surprises on the day. The games run in a modern web browser, so you can put the link on the board or share it with families. If you are choosing between the two games for a class, the games page explains the difference. Backrooms has a spookier atmosphere, so most teachers will want to start with Vocabulario Español. The Spanish learning guides are a good companion for students who want the grammar behind the words, and the vocabulary list shows every word in alphabetical order.
