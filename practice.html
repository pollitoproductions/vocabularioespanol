Practice the words

Flashcards, a quiz and an el/la trainer built from the same 1,000 words as the game. Free, no sign-up.

Why practice this way

Seeing a word once in the game is a good start, but memory grows through repeated, active recall. That means trying to produce the meaning before you look at it. The flashcards, the quiz and the el/la trainer on this page all work that way. They use the same 1,000 words from Vocabulario Español, so anything you collected in the game can be tested here, and anything you miss here is a good reason to go back and find it again in the world.

How to use the three modes

Flashcards show the picture and the Spanish word. Say it aloud, think of the meaning, then tap the card to check. Mark the ones you knew and the ones you did not; the cards you missed come back around first, and the cards you know are remembered on this device so tomorrow's session starts where today's finished. You can flip the direction so you see English and have to recall Spanish, which is harder and more useful for speaking.

Quiz gives you a word and four choices. The wrong answers are real words from the list, so you have to know the meaning rather than guess by elimination. Quiz yourself in both directions: Spanish to English tests recognition, which is what reading and listening need, while English to Spanish tests recall, which is what speaking and writing need.

El or la? is a short drill on gender. In Spanish every noun is masculine or feminine, and the article is the quickest clue. The only dependable way to learn the gender is to learn the noun together with its article, so this mode shows you a noun without it and asks you to choose. A few words, like el agua or el águila, look like exceptions but follow a sound rule; the guide on el and la explains why.

A simple routine

Ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Try a round of twenty flashcards, then a twenty-question quiz on the same words, and finish by playing the game for a while and listening for the words you just practiced. Keep the cards you miss in rotation until they feel boring, then move on. If you are a teacher or a parent, the printable worksheets on the resources page pair well with this page for work away from the screen, and the guides explain the grammar behind the words. Nothing here needs an account, and your progress never leaves your browser.
