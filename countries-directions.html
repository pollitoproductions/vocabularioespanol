Spanish-speaking countries and directions

Many of the places in Vocabulario Español are Spanish-speaking countries, and the game also teaches the words you need to say which way to go. This guide puts them together.

Notice how country names are written. In Spanish they are capitalized, just as in English, but languages and nationalities are not: España is the country, while español is the language and un español is a person from Spain. Some names include an article, such as los Estados Unidos and El Salvador.

Direction words in the game

  • arriba — up
  • abajo — down
  • izquierda — left
  • derecha — right

To say “to the left” or “to the right,” add a la: a la izquierda, a la derecha. To say “straight ahead,” people say todo recto in Spain and derecho in much of Latin America. The compass directions are norte, sur, este, and oeste.

Asking for and giving directions

Try these short exchanges:

  • ¿Dónde está el mercado? — Está a la izquierda. (Where is the market? It is on the left.)
  • ¿Dónde está la escuela? — Está a la derecha. (Where is the school? It is on the right.)
  • Voy arriba. / Voy abajo. (I’m going up. / I’m going down.)

Notice that estar is used to say where something is located.

Grouping the countries by region

  • Europe and Africa: España and Guinea Ecuatorial (Equatorial Guinea), the only independent country in Africa where Spanish is an official language
  • Mexico and Central America: México, Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panamá
  • The Caribbean: Cuba, República Dominicana, Puerto Rico
  • South America: Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Perú, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay

Saying where you are from

Use ser de for where someone is from and vivir en for where they live: ¿De dónde eres? Soy de México. Vivo en Chile. The nationality changes with gender: mexicano / mexicana, argentino / argentina, chileno / chilena, colombiano / colombiana, peruano / peruana, cubano / cubana, español / española. A person from the United States is estadounidense, which has one form for everyone.

Longer directions

Once you know the basics, you can connect them: Siga derecho y gire a la izquierda means “Keep going straight and turn left.” That is the polite form you would use with a stranger. With a friend, you would say Sigue derecho y gira a la izquierda. Try writing directions from your classroom to another room using these words.

Practice ideas

  • As you explore the game, say out loud which direction you are going, such as Voy a la derecha.
  • Pick a country from the list and write one sentence: Quiero visitar Chile (I want to visit Chile).
  • Sketch a simple map of your room and label it with the direction words.
